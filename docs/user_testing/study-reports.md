# Study Reports

## Study Report 1

On the login page, the user was a little unsure what Commons was, but he eventually figured out how to create an account. His first task was to add some of the characters that he already knows to the studied collection, but my task didn't explicitly mention the studied collection. He was a little confused about how to track learned characters in the app; the idea of using a collection was likely not intuitive. For the next task, he was to figure out a way to speed up adding all characters from a popular resource to a collection. He was a little confused by the instructions and did not know that the public collections page would solve this. When it was time to share a collection with someone, he looked in a lot of different places, such as the public collections and the "Shared with me" page, before finally finding it. I’m surprised that this is not as intuitive to find; perhaps I need to add a more explicit button on the home page to share collections with others. There was unexpected behavior for one task: keeping track of characters to study later by difficulty level. He initially approached this by creating collections for a hard difficulty and then adding characters to it. While adding the characters, he realized that there is a personal rating option for each character. He eventually used that, which was the expected behavior, but still used the collections. The personal rating option might not be obvious at first and considering that collections are useful for categorizing characters, it makes sense for users to use them to assign difficulties. To fix this, I could make the personal rating option stand out more. However, he was able to solve some tasks pretty quickly, such as checking a character's definition and discovering the filtering options.
